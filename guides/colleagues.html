The Eton French Apps: Quick Start for Colleagues

Modern Languages and Cultures · October 2026

Like all of my other resources, these are optional. The core material is completely unchanged and you’re welcome to carry on teaching it in your own way and in your own style without needing to use these new resources at all.

I designed them with a view to making language learning more engaging, more productive and more accessible to boys of all abilities.

It means that different configurations of vocabulary and grammar can be built in and tests can be set at different levels, while still keeping the rigour of the original resources that I created many years ago.

Naturally, anyone who creates resources builds them around the way they themselves enjoy teaching, and these are no exception. Of course you are equally free to make your own resources, based on the same core material, that suit your own way of teaching better.

What they are

A set of web pages for vocabulary, verbs, grammar, listening and oral practice that I have made for our boys and for us to use when we want to. They work in all browsers and, most recently, have been adapted to work in the new Prisma browser. They are designed for the iPad and the projector. There is nothing to install and no log-in. Open the page, choose what to practise, and go.

Where they came from

It began in summer 2026, after a conversation at a leavers’ dinner, where someone was raving about what Claude could do. I fed in some of my existing vocabulary sheets to see what would happen, and that produced a first, rather jittery, working shell.

I tried it with some groups of boys towards the end of the Summer Half. Parts of it worked well and the boys gained some benefit, but it was not ready to roll out: the features it has now were things I had not yet imagined, and the teething problems only showed up when boys used it in front of me in School. Fixes sometimes caused new problems, and with too much School time going on fielding errors and too few lessons left to test properly, I put the project aside over the summer holidays.

I did not pick it up again until late September, when Claude seemed to have got much better at the job. I committed fully for a month, paying the maximum for the most powerful subscription and, when even that ran out of tokens, buying more, because I was so impressed by the progress I had made and became determined to see the project through. There have been well over 800 recorded changes since that restart in late September.

Over the last couple of weeks I have been trying them out more and more with some of my Divs. There were still some complete disasters to put right, such as tests crashing and links not working. All of those have now been fixed, and I am happy for them to be rolled out and excited to be able to share them with you, although there may still be the odd small problem.

Three ways to use them

  1. In School. Put an app on the projector and use a drill or a quick test as a starter or plenary.
  2. As a link for the boys. Choose the vocabulary list or grammar recap you want in an app, tap the copy button and paste the link into your Div’s Class Notebook (OneNote) or Teams. A boy who taps it lands straight on that content with the drill, test and speed buttons ready.
  3. As a short test. In an app, start a Spot Test (Eton Spot Test: timed & submitted), then tap Make a link to share this test. Paste the link where your boys will find it. They do it on the iPad with the Apple Pencil, mark it themselves and hand it in as a PDF. Each link gives every boy his own draw of the questions.

The material has not changed

The content is exactly what you already know. Only the way the boys practise it is new.

  • Grammar EWs (non-specialists and A Level). The recaps and exercises follow the same grammar prescription as the pamphlets. The exercises themselves are new, written to that same prescription, so they replace the old pre-test exercises without changing what is being taught.
  • IGCSE vocabulary EWs. These are the same vocabulary prescriptions as before, with the same set names.

Differentiation

  • Vocabulary is graded. Every word is graded by difficulty, and you or the boys can sort and choose by it: Easy looks like English (famille), Medium looks like a different English word (appartement), Hard gives nothing to go on (manger). A stronger boy can go straight to the Hard words, and a weaker one can build confidence on the Easy ones.
  • Grammar is graded in the same way. Every sentence has been rated on its own for how hard the grammar is to get right: the trickiest thing in that sentence (an exception, an agreement, a pronoun order, a tense or mood choice). Length does not decide the level.
    • Easy is a straightforward application of the point.
    • Medium needs a careful choice or an irregular form.
    • Hard has a real trap, or several linked decisions at once. Early chapters have few Hard sentences and later chapters have more.
    The different kinds of exercise (rules, translations, transforms, gaps, spot the error, rebuild) are graded as well, so you can set an easier or a harder test on the same grammar point for different boys in the same Div.

A Level Specialist Vocabulary (the old VVs)

This covers the old VVs, still organised by topic (Topics 1 to 12) in weekly sets, for example TV1a, TV1b, TV1c. What is new is how each week is divided, so that the topic vocabulary and the verb structures can be used separately or together, and the verb structures are now graded by importance:

  • Topic vocabulary of the week.
  • Verb structures of the week: Essential (the five most important, to be written in French), Important (the next five) and Recognition (all the others, to be understood).

The default for every boy is the topic vocabulary plus the five Essential verbs. That is the minimum each boy learns for his EW, and the app opens on it. Compared with learning the whole set, it cuts the workload by about 40%. It is also what tests in School should be based on: all of the topic vocabulary and the five core verbs.

Gently encouraging more. The core keeps the EW workload down in the busy Half, but it cannot limit what a boy meets in the exam. The vocabulary in the full lists is what boys are likely to meet, to a greater or lesser degree, so a boy who can bolt on the Important verbs, and some of the others, is giving himself a real advantage on the day. It works well to say so now and then: the core is the minimum we test, and stretching beyond it is for the boy who feels able to.

IGCSE vocabulary EWs

Each weekly list (about 90 words) is graded by how useful a word is to be able to produce in the IGCSE: about 20 Essential, about 25 Important, and the rest Recognition. The app keeps this separate from the Easy, Medium and Hard levels, which are about how hard a word is to remember: Easy words are cognates, which look like English (famille), while Hard words give you nothing to go on (manger).

  • The default: learn the Essential and Important words (about 45) from English into French, and the Recognition words from French into English. Every word is still met, but only the most useful ones have to be produced.
  • For stronger boys: aim to learn the whole list from English into French.
  • For the boys who need the lightest load: the Essential words alone (about 20) from English into French, and the rest from French into English.
