Part 1. Read this first: how every Eton app works

All of the Eton apps share the same look, the same buttons and the same keys. Learn them once here and you will know them everywhere. Later parts describe each app on its own, grouped by level and then by language.

1.1 Getting into an app

  • Each app is one web page. Your teacher gives you the link (usually in OneNote or Teams). Open it in your browser on your iPad or computer.

  • Each page name starts with Eton. The exact page name and link are printed at the top of every app's entry in this guide.

  • If your teacher publishes a newer version, the page refreshes itself once. Your saved answers are kept.

  • Tests that your teacher sets you need an internet connection. Pages that only drill are happy once they have loaded.

  • Tap 🔊 Voices (top of every app) to check which voice reads each language. On an iPad the voice sounds far better if you download it first: Settings > Accessibility > Spoken Content > Voices.

  • Tap ⌂ Home to go back to the start screen. It stops any audio.

  • Tap ← Back (next to Home, once you have left the first screen) to go back exactly one step, for example from a drill to its set-up screen, or from a set-up screen to the menu. It also stops any audio.

1.2 The set-up screen (the "Ready to practise?" page)

Almost every app opens on a set-up screen. It works in two steps, and nothing is chosen for you.

  • FIRST · What do you want to practise? The activities are in three groups, easy to hard (the label on each card says Easy, Medium, Harder or Hardest). Words and structures: Key words (the useful vocabulary of this particular demo, with the sentence it came from), Top Structures (the Top Structures the answer uses, English to French or Italian) and Bold bits (only the highlighted Top Structure in a demo sentence: say what it means, or say it). Build sentences: Gaps (a short phrase is missing from a demo sentence), Word order (put the chunks of a demo sentence back in order; the capital letter marks the start) and Spot the mistake (one slip in a sentence from the demo: a missing or wrong word, or a small spelling or grammar error. There is no clue: find it and write only the corrected word or words, not the whole sentence; if a word is extra, write delete and that word). Translate: short sentences from the demo, with the key structures given as clues. Tick one, or several together. Every activity gives you at least ten items for each demo.

  • Clues. A clue never appears by itself. In a Drill, tap 💡 Give me a clue if you need one, before you show the answer: it gives a few dashes and the number of letters, the English meaning or the demo sentence, the start of the sentence, or the Top Structures to use. Spot the mistake has no clue at all. A clue never shows by itself anywhere: in a Test, a timed test and the Speed Challenge you tap 💡 Give me a clue on the item you are stuck on, and on a printed sheet the clues sit in their own box at the foot of each activity, which stays off unless you tick Show the clues. If you would rather do it without help, open Settings and switch Clues and hints off.

  • NEXT · How do you want to practise it? Six picture tiles, each with a line saying what it does: Drill, ⚡ Speed Challenge, Spell, Match, ☕ Leisure Mode and Test. Press the one you want. Above the tiles is a gold ⚙ Settings bar that says in a few words what you have set (for example "10 cards · 8s a card"). Tap Change to open it.

The controls have the same names everywhere:

1.2a ★ My personalised set, ✍ Spell and 🧩 Match

These are on the set-up screens of the IGCSE vocabulary, Gr EWs, Approved Material, Top Structures and verb apps.

  • ★ Personalised set. Every card you mark ✗ Major error in a drill, or in Spell, joins your personalised set automatically. ~ Nearly just brings the card back a few cards later: if it still beats you, mark it ✗. Under FIRST · What do you want to practise? tap ★ My personalised set, then choose Drill, Spell, Match, Leisure Mode or Test exactly as you would on any other set. Once it is chosen you can narrow it to Hard only, Tricky + hard or Due today. The Speed Challenge works on whole lists, so it is not offered for the personalised set.

  • ? How does this work? sits beside ★ My personalised set and gives a short explanation in plain words.

  • ✎ Edit lists your cards. Tap the level (Hard, Tricky, Easier) to change it, or tap ✕ to take a card out.

  • Due today. A card you missed comes back after 1 day, then 3 days, then 7 days. Get it right each time and it stops coming back.

  • 🔗 Save my personalised set gives you a link. Press Copy link and paste it into your own OneNote, under a clear title, and keep that page tidy. Tap that link on any device and the app opens with your set ready. Your set lives only on this iPad, in this browser. If the browser data is cleared, or you use another device, it is gone. The link is the only safe copy, and looking after it is up to you. Paste a fresh link after each big session.

  • ✍ Spell. The app says the phrase aloud. Type what you hear, or write it in the box with your Apple Pencil (iPadOS turns your handwriting into text; if nothing happens, check that Settings, Apple Pencil, Scribble is on), then press Check. Words in green are right, wavy orange is an accent slip, red is wrong or missing. Accents count. Then mark yourself with the usual ✗ Major error ← · ~ Nearly ↓ · ✓ Got it → row. Use 🔊 Listen to hear it again and Show English if you need to check you have understood it — useful for a longer sentence, where it is really a short translation as much as a spelling exercise. It uses whichever set you have chosen, and the number of cards you picked in Settings. In the Approved Material app a long sentence is a long dictation: take it in pieces and press Listen as often as you need.

  • 🧩 Match. Tap an English card, then its foreign pair. A wrong pair adds 3 seconds. The app shows your time and your best time on this iPad. There are six pairs (four in the Approved Material app, where the phrases are longer). It is a good five-minute warm-up for a Div. Match opens with a two-second picture splash with a quick arcade power-up sound (the 🔊 button turns it off), and the same picture glows faintly behind the game. Each matched pair turns its own colour and makes a soft pop, a wrong pair shakes and adds the 3 seconds, and the timer turns orange and then red as it climbs. Each game opens with a different picture across the top, and a little ghost 👻 races along a bar at your best time, so you can see whether you are ahead of it. Matching several pairs in a row builds a streak, you earn up to three stars at the end, and a new best time gets a celebration. The pairs are not read aloud unless you tap 🗣 Voice off to switch the voice on. The 🔊 button turns the sound effects off.

1.3 The self-marking drill

This is the heart of every app. You mark yourself, so be honest. Nobody can see your score and nobody is fooled by an inflated one.

  1. Choose your set, then press the Drill tile. A card appears and the question is read aloud. A timer bar shrinks.

  2. Say the answer out loud (or write it). Do this before you look.

  3. Press Show Answer, or press →. The answer appears and is read aloud. Press 🔊 Replay to hear it again. If the timer runs out the answer appears by itself ("Time's up!").

  4. Mark yourself with the three buttons or the arrow keys (table below).

  5. At the end you get your score. Cards you marked as major errors come back for a second go.

Mark Key What happens
✗ Major error ← You did not know it, or it was badly wrong. The card is saved on your error list and comes back in the Major Error Drill.
~ Nearly ↓ Close, with a small slip (an accent, an ending). The card comes back 2 or 3 cards later in the same drill.
✓ Got it → Right. It counts as a point.

Showing the answer: press → (in most apps Space or Enter also work). Then ←, ↓ and → mark the card.

  • The bar at the top shows your running score (✓, ~ and ✗ counts).

  • ▶ Errors opens your error list so far.

  • ⚙ Adjust (where you see it) pauses the drill so you can change the direction or the number of cards. Press ▶ Apply & resume to carry on.

  • In a verb Drill the infinitive is hidden, so you practise the conjugation without leaning on it. The 💡 Infinitive button in the top bar switches it On or Off at any moment, even in the middle of a card.

  • In the Major Error Drill you can also press ⏭ Ignore to skip a card with no penalty.

The end screen

  • Your score as x / total and a percentage.

  • Either "No major errors: clean sweep" or "N cards to revisit". Copy list copies those cards so you can paste them into OneNote.

  • ▶ Drill major errors again, ↻ Do same set again (all the cards of your selection, not just the first few) and ⌂ Home.

Tip Use Nearly honestly. It is the button that brings a card back quickly, and that repetition is what makes the card stick.

1.4 Leisure Mode (☕)

Leisure Mode is for soaking up the language. Make your selection, press ☕ Leisure Mode and sit back. Each card is read aloud, there is a pause for you to answer in your head or out loud, then the answer is shown and read, and the next card follows. It loops for ever, reshuffling. There is no marking, no score and no error list.

  • Thinking time: starts at 4 seconds. The − and + buttons speed it up or slow it down while it plays; the change applies from the next pause.

  • ⏸ Pause / ▶ Resume: or press Space.

  • 🔊 Replay repeats the current card. ⌂ Home stops.

Tip Leisure Mode is excellent in the evening, on a walk or on a journey. Try answering out loud before the answer arrives.

1.5 ⚡ Eton Speed Challenge (the tile game)

A fast game for pairs or teams. It fills the screen with numbered tiles. Each tile shows a prompt (usually English). Open it with the ⚡ Speed Challenge tile on the set-up screen.

The bar under the tiles is in three groups: New set, Pause timer, Reveal all and How to play first; the round settings (Level, Tiles, Time, Teams and so on) in the middle, under a This round label; and Print sheet, Sound on and the test buttons last, under More tools.

  1. Choose the settings: Level (Easy / Medium / Hard / Mixed), Tiles (6, 9, 12 or 16), Time, Teams (Off, 2, 3 or 4; click a team name to rename it), Each turn (20 seconds to 1 minute) and Direction. If your list has fewer items than the number of tiles, a short note says so and you get one tile for each item.

  2. Say the number of a tile and then say the answer aloud.

  3. Click the tile (or type its number) to reveal and hear the correct answer.

  4. Press ✓ Right (the tile greys out and you score a point) or ✗ Wrong (a new item replaces it).

  5. Beeps sound at one third and two thirds of the time. A buzzer sounds at the end.

  6. In team games, a wrong answer passes the turn to the next team at once, and a flashing sign shows whose go it is. The team with the most tiles wins.

When the game ends you can ▶ Play the N left on screen, ✗ Play the N that were wrong, 📋 Go through the wrong ones (no timer) or deal a 🔀 New full set.

Other buttons: ❓ How to play, 🔀 New set, ⏸ Pause timer, 👁 Reveal all, 🖨 Print sheet, 🔊 Sound on. The game also has its own No-limit test and Spot Test buttons that use the tiles you were playing with.

A Speed session is the same game but set by your teacher through a link. It is locked to your teacher's choice, starts with 2 teams, 16 tiles, English to the foreign language, 20 seconds a turn and a 3 minute game, and you stay in it until your teacher says stop. There is no leaving it.

1.6 Tests you can make yourself

Choose your set, then press the Test tile on the set-up screen. The choices sit in one purple Spot Test card:

Where What it gives you
The big yellow ✍ Do it with Apple Pencil button The Spot Test done on screen with your Apple Pencil, marked by you and handed in as a PDF if required. This is the one to use for serious practice. See 1.7.
The small link Prefer paper? Print it to write on by hand. The same Spot Test as a sheet of 5 boxes with the questions printed on the left and room for your answers on the right. Every question is marked on its own: 2 for perfection, 1 for 75% or more correct, 0 otherwise. You add up the marks yourself: the total is 2 × the number of questions (15 questions is out of 30). The answers are behind a Show answers button.
The discreet link No-limit test (up to the whole set) ▸ at the bottom of the card A printable test of as many questions as you like. You choose 10, 20, 25 or Custom and press Make the test (in the verb apps there is also a Sentences tab; in the Gr EWs apps the link opens the grammar test options directly). The answers stay hidden until you press Show answers. Use Copy test sheet, Copy answer sheet or 🖨 Print / PDF. Good for homework or testing a friend. If your browser blocks the new print window, the sheet appears on the screen instead: tap Print / Save as PDF at the top, or Close to go back.

A Spot Test normally has 15 short questions in 5 boxes (3 per box). The Items per box chips let you choose 5, 10 or 15. You may get fewer if the set is small, or if the answers are long: a timed test never runs past 4½ minutes (the card says so). Grammar (Gr EWs) also starts on 15. When a test panel is open it stands alone on the page with just its Close button, and the header Back button closes it first.

1.7 The timed “Spot Test” with the Apple Pencil, step by step

  1. Press the big yellow ✍ Do it with Apple Pencil button. A box asks you to Choose your level. Leave it on Mixed unless your teacher has told you otherwise. Your level is printed at the top of your PDF.

  2. Choose ⏱ Timed (use this in Birley Schools, or when your teacher has set the test as an EW) or 🏠 Untimed. You cannot change your mind once you start. The clock starts the moment you tap. It can be paused only for a real emergency (a fire alarm, for example): press ⏸ Pause at the top right, type (or write with your Apple Pencil) why you are pausing, then press ▶ Resume. While paused the clock is stopped and the test is hidden, you must still stay on the page (leaving it still sounds the error buzz and is logged), and every pause is printed on your PDF. While a timed test is open your iPad stays awake (it will not dim or lock itself); if you lock it yourself with the side button, that is logged like leaving the page. If the clock at the top of the screen distracts you, tap it to make it smaller (tap again to make it bigger); it stays on screen. Blank answers score 0. No timed test runs longer than 4½ minutes: a test of short answers (words or verb forms) can have up to 20 items, while longer answers give fewer. Where your teacher's list gives more than one accepted answer for an English prompt (for example "living room": la salle de séjour / le salon), the mark scheme now shows them all. Spellings that are right either way are listed too (for example l'aller simple, with or without the hyphen).

  3. Answer each item. Each has two tabs: ✍️ Handwrite (Apple Pencil, black ink) and ⌨️ Type. Only type if you type in exams or have no Pencil.

  4. When finished, press 🔒 I've attempted all N: lock in. In a timed test you get 30 seconds of grace after the time ends, and then your answers lock themselves.

  5. Press ✔ Show answers & mark. Correct your own answer in red first (tick it if perfect, or underline the errors). You cannot choose a mark until there is red on the answer. Once you have used red pen, the app reminds you in red to tap a mark. If those red reminders are too much, press 🔕 Turn off the red “tap your mark” alerts (it appears the first time, or in the marking box); press 🔔 Turn the red alerts back on to bring them back. Your choice is remembered on that iPad. Without the alerts, an unmarked answer still scores 0.

  6. Now give yourself a mark. Single words and forms: 2 Right, 1 Nearly (a missing accent, a spelling slip, a wrong ending or gender), 0 Wrong. Sentences and structures: 4 Perfect, 3 Mostly right, 2 About half right, 1 Only a word or two right, 0 Nothing right. The test is marked out of 20. In a timed test the marking time is limited too, and anything you have not marked scores 0. If you finish marking early, tap I've finished marking: after an Are you sure? check, the marking timer stops and you go straight on to What I learned.

  7. Write in the 📝 What I learned box. This is the most important part: what caught you out, and how will you remember it? In Birley Schools you get a couple of minutes; in your House take as long as you need. Try to find 10 things to write (5 is the minimum), numbered 1 to 10: even a single piece of vocabulary or a rule counts. The PDF button unlocks only after you have written for 1 minute.

  8. Press 📄 Save my work as PDF and file it (see 1.9).

Careful Do not leave the page during a test. If you switch to another app or tab, use Split View, reload or press Back, an error buzz sounds and a red warning flashes. The attempt is logged and recorded, and the PDF says how many times you left and for how long.

If you are not sure about a mark, do not stop to ask your teacher. Mark it wrong if in doubt and ask afterwards.

  • Your attempt number is shown ("ATTEMPT n ON THIS TEST").

  • If a page reloads by accident, reopening the same test within 12 hours picks up where you were. There is a Start afresh option.

  • When you have finished, marked and saved your PDF, you can press 🔄 Take a fresh test at the bottom of the page for new questions with the same settings. The test you have just done is cleared, so save its PDF first. Teacher-set tests do not have this button.

1.8 Writing with the Apple Pencil (Eton PEN)

  • Written answers have two tabs: ✍️ Handwrite (the default) and ⌨️ Type.

  • Pen tools: Pen, Thin / Medium, Eraser, Undo, Clear and + space (adds more room).

  • When the app detects your Pencil, your finger no longer draws: it scrolls the page.

  • Write in black ink. Once marking begins the black ink locks, and a 🖊 Red pen: corrections appears. All corrections go in red. The eraser then removes red only.

  • Typed answers get a red "Corrections" box instead.

1.9 Saving your work to OneNote

Marked papers and timed tests end with 📄 Save my work as PDF (or 📄 Save marked paper as PDF).

  1. The app asks for your name once and remembers it. It also gives you a OneNote page title (date, then a clear title such as "Spot Test on VV10a") with a Copy button.

  2. On an iPad the print screen opens. Do not print. Tap the Share button, then OneNote.

  3. Choose your Div's Class OneNote, then your name, then Class Work (work done in Birley Schools) or EW (work done in your House).

  4. Paste the title you copied, then press Send.

  5. In OneNote, tap the PDF icon and choose Printout so that the pages show in full. (The paper-clip File button only adds a small attachment box.)

On a computer: Save as PDF, then in OneNote choose Insert > File Printout. If the share route fails on an iPad, use Save to Files, then in OneNote Insert > PDF.

1.10 Grammar recaps and "Now I can" checklists

Every grammar chapter in the Gr EWs apps has an Eton grammar recap: a clear explanation of the rules, with examples. Read the recap before you drill the chapter. All recaps use the same colours, and a colour key sits at the top of each one:

Colour What it means
Core rule (blue) The rule itself.
Examples & how to say (green) Examples, with how to say them.
Tips & patterns (amber) Tips and patterns that help you remember.
Exceptions: learn by heart (red) The awkward ones. Learn them.
Watch out (yellow) The points to be careful about.
Lists & vocab (white) Lists and vocabulary to learn.

Each recap also has two special boxes:

  • Soon I will be able to… (grey dashed box, near the top): what you are about to learn. It is for reading, and cannot be ticked.

  • Now I can… (bright yellow box at the very end): a checklist for after you have drilled the chapter. Tick each box when you can do it, then press the red Check button to compare with a model answer (the button then says Hide). The prompts are in English. The French or Italian appears only in the model answer.

Your ticks are saved on that device and in that browser only. They do not go to your teacher, they do not show on your other devices, and clearing the site data wipes them. They do not print either. You find the recaps in the IGCSE app (section 2.3, Vocab and Gr EWs).

Links from your teacher. If your teacher pastes a link to a grammar recap (for example into OneNote), tap it and the recap for the right chapter or chapters opens straight away, with the live Now I can… boxes and their Check buttons. At the top of that page is a Practise this grammar bar with Drill, Test, Spot Test and Speed Challenge buttons, already set up for those chapters. A link to a vocab list works the same way: tap it and the app opens with exactly that list chosen, with the six practice tiles ready (Drill, Speed Challenge, Spell, Match, Leisure Mode, Test), so you do not have to choose the list again.

Tip Be strict. Tick a box only if you could do it from memory, then press Check to see whether you really could.

1.11 Things to ignore

Every app contains tools that belong to your teacher. You will never need them, and you should leave them alone:

  • The Eton crest in the header (tapping it just says "Not available").

  • Any button marked ✎ Edit, ✏️ Edit Vocab, Print Content, Study Sheet, ⬇ Export HTML or 👩‍🏫 Teacher.

  • Any button starting 🔑 (making test links). You will not even see these unless your teacher has set up a key on that device.

2.1 French

Eton French Verbs

▶ Open Eton French Verbs (opens the app in your browser)

What it is for: drilling, gap-fill sentences and tests on French verbs. The subtitle reads "Conjugation drills · Sentences · Tests · Audio". The first screen offers two big doors: 100 Core Verbs (the 100 core verbs and the four Core Exercises) and All Verbs (Specialist Verbs, Specialist Verb Structures, or Both). Tap one to open its set-up screen. The ‹ French Verbs link at the top, and ← Back, take you back to the two doors. Both doors use the same two steps.

FIRST · What do you want to practise? Nothing is chosen for you. Choose your verbs, then the kind of question: Single form, Full (6 forms), Infinitives, Sentences or (on 100 Core Verbs) Trials-style exercises. Then tick the tenses, numbered in the order we learn them. The six practice tiles stay greyed out until you have chosen verbs and at least one tense. On 100 Core Verbs you either Choose my own (tick the verb groups: Essential 1 is the first 20 verbs, Essential 2 the second 20, Important the next 30, Useful the last 30; tap See the verbs in these groups to read exactly which verbs they are; Perfect: avoir verbs only leaves out the verbs that take être) or Use a Trials set (press F Michaelmas Trials, E Block Michaelmas Trials, F Block Summer Trials or E Block Summer Trials and every choice is made for you and locked in; Choose my own instead unlocks it). A gold-edged line always says in words what you are about to practise. Under All Verbs, the Verb set strip switches between Specialist Verbs, Both and Specialist Verb Structures.

NEXT · How do you want to practise it? Press a tile: Drill, ⚡ Speed Challenge, Spell, Match, ☕ Leisure Mode or Test. The gold ⚙ Settings bar (tap Change) holds Cards per session, Timer per card, Autotime, Hint, Regularity and Person, and on All Verbs also Importance and Direction. On 100 Core Verbs, two more tiles sit after the six: 📝 Practice Trials paper and 📖 View & Print. The Eton Speed Challenge has its own set-up screen once it opens; it starts on your verb set, tenses (and infinitives, if ticked) and Importance choice, and you can change them there.

Choose a verb set

  • 📚 100 Core Verbs: 100 verbs in four groups (Essential 1: 20, Essential 2: 20, Important: 30, Useful: 30), all six tenses plus compound tenses. This is the default.

  • 🎓 Specialist Verbs: 35 less common verbs (acquérir, naître, croître, mentir, peindre, valoir, croire, tenir, mettre, mourir, s'asseoir…).

  • ⚡ Both: the two together.

  • 🧩 Specialist Verb Structures: 586 Eton Advanced Verb Structures, set phrases such as "profiter de X", conjugated in every tense. Tick Display structure hint to see the French structure as well as the English.

  • 🏅 F Michaelmas Trials: all the 100 Core Verbs, for the Michaelmas trials. Only three tenses are offered: Infinitive, Present and Perfect, and the Perfect has only the verbs that take avoir.

  • 🏅 E Block Michaelmas Trials: all the 100 Core Verbs, with Infinitive, Present, Perfect (every verb, including être and reflexive verbs), Imperfect and Future.

  • 🏅 F Block Summer Trials and 🏅 E Block Summer Trials: the same verbs and tenses as E Block Michaelmas Trials.

Use Specialist Verbs and Specialist Verb Structures when Eton tells you to. The Importance buttons (Essential, Important, Useful) work inside these four sets too, so you can sort the trials verbs by importance, and 📖 View & Print can group them by tier.

The numbered questions. Inside FIRST the questions are numbered in the order to answer them: 1 Which verbs, 2 Which tenses, 3 Kind of question and, for Trials-style exercises, 4 Which exercises. They unlock one at a time: until you have ticked your verbs, questions 2, 3 and 4 are greyed out with a note saying what to answer first, and so on down the list (a Trials set answers 1 and 2 for you). Nothing is hidden; you just take the questions in order. A yellow line tells you what is still to choose, and the NEXT buttons wake up once everything is answered.

How important is each verb? Under Which verbs (FIRST, Choose my own) there are four tick buttons: Essential 1 (20), Essential 2 (20), Important (30) and Useful (30). The Top Structure Cycle

The coloured panel beside a card is a checklist of structures to build into your answers. It has four groups: Easy Present Tense Starter (green, 8 prompts), Past Tense Anecdote (blue, 18), Detailed Opinion (purple, 22) and Future Speculation (red, 14). Tap a prompt and a sheet opens with five example phrases, each with 🔊. Use it to make sure every answer has a present, a past, an opinion and a future.

Learn from this demo

On a card with a model answer, 🎓 Learn from this demo turns the model answer into practice, one small step at a time. Listen to the demo first (🎧 Demo Mode), then press the button. It has two steps, like the set-up screens elsewhere in the app.

  • FIRST · What do you want to practise? Tick any of: Top Structures (the Top Structures this answer uses, English to French or Italian), Gaps (a structure is missing from a sentence of the demo: fill it in), Word order (put the jumbled words of a demo sentence back in order), Spot the mistake (one slip in a sentence from the demo, with no clue: find it and write only the corrected word or words, not the whole sentence) and Translate (sentences from the demo, short and long). The dots show how hard each one is, from easiest at the top. Tick one, or several together. Every activity gives you at least ten items for each demo. Spot the mistake is deliberately subtle: a word missing (a reflexive pronoun, a preposition, an article), a real word used in the wrong place, a small spelling, accent or hyphen slip, or a quiet grammar slip. After you answer it shows the correct sentence and a short reason.

  • NEXT · How do you want to practise it? Choose one of Drill (items one at a time, you mark yourself), Match (pair up English and French, six at a time, against the clock with your best time to beat, from the Key words, Top Structures and Bold bits you ticked), ⚡ Speed Challenge (race the clock), Test (on screen), 🖨 Print a sheet (to write on paper), Read it again or Have a go (use the structures in a real oral practice session). Once you tick an activity, Read it again and Have a go are greyed out, because they work on the whole demo. Untick everything to use them.

Translate 10 sentences

  1. Press 🎓 Learn from this demo, then ✍️ Translate 10 sentences (only on cards that have a demo). Translate each English sentence with the Pencil in black ink, or type.

  2. A gold chip on each sentence names the structure to use. Sentences 1 to 6 are from the demo. Sentences 7 to 10 are "Make it your own": one thing has been changed.

  3. Choose a mode: ⚡ Quick 5: 6 minutes (5 sentences, 2 minutes marking, out of 20), ⏱ Timed: 10 minutes, or 🏠 Untimed. In Quick 5 and Timed you can use ⏸ Pause (top right) only for a real emergency: write why, then press ▶ Resume. The pause is printed on your PDF.

  4. Press 🔒 I've attempted all N: lock in, then ✔ Show answers & mark. Correct in red, then mark each sentence 4 Perfect, 3 Mostly right, 2 About half right, 1 Only a word or two right, 0 Nothing right. It is out of 40 (20 in Quick 5).

  5. Write your What I learned notes (in Quick 5, One thing I'll remember), then save the PDF. The error buzz and logging apply here too.

Record my oral (EW)

Careful Your answers must be completely your own and completely different from the Demo Mode answers. Do not listen to the demo before you record. The demo is offered only after you have saved your recording.

  1. Press 🎙 Record my oral (EW). Read the checklist: quiet room, iPad an arm's length away, volume up, use the iPad speaker (no headphones), do not lock the screen or leave the page. Type your name.

  2. Press 🔈 Test my microphone and say a few words while you watch the level meter. If the bar does not move after a few seconds, the app tries other settings by itself. If it still shows no bar, press 🎧 Record 4 seconds and play back, say a few words and press play. If you hear your voice, the microphone works. If you hear nothing, press 🔧 I hear nothing: try other settings. If you see "Nothing was recorded", check Settings > your browser > Microphone, reload the page and try again.

  3. Press ● Start recording. The five questions are asked aloud and timed. On an iPad the recording pauses while the examiner is speaking, so it keeps only your answers. The questions are printed on your PDF (the set-up box may still say they will be on the recording; on an iPad they will not). The REC clock stands still while the examiner talks, so the finished length is shorter than the real time. You need Safari on the iPad.

  4. If you did not catch a question, press 🔁 Hear the question again in the red bar (or 🔁 Repeat question in the panel). The questions, the View all questions buttons and the Top Structure Cycle are hidden during a recording, as in the real exam.

  5. If you leave the page while recording and come back, a flashing red warning tells you to stay on the page. There is no error buzz and nothing is logged.

  6. ■ Stop and throw away ends the recording and deletes it. The yellow ■ Stop and check recording ends it straight away for a quick check only ("You stopped early, so this is only a quick check"). Use it to test your microphone, never for your real recording.

  7. At the end a player appears: listen back (on an iPad you hear your answers only). 🔁 Not happy: try again, or 📤 Share recording or 💾 Save to Files. The file is named with the date, the card and your name.

  8. Press 📄 Make my PDF (card, questions and the time each started) and follow the OneNote guide on screen: a new page in the EW folder, the PDF as a Printout and the recording added with the paper-clip File button.

This is for the whole Div. It has two tabs. Choose structures with 📚 Choose structures, pick a language, and use 🎲 Random card or a card code.

Part 3. Getting the most from the apps

A simple weekly routine

  1. Learn the material for your set (vocab list, VV set, Gr EW chapter or Approved Material topic) with Read & listen, View & Print or Leisure Mode.

  2. Drill it out loud: say the answer before you press Show. Mark honestly.

  3. Re-drill your errors with ▶ Drill major errors again.

  4. Test yourself with ✍ Spot Test. Write your What I learned notes properly.

  5. File the PDF in OneNote, in the right folder, with the title copied from the app.
