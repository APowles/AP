Overview of F – D for Masters: what stays the same and what has changed

What stays the same

The scheme of work, the weekly rhythm of Schools and EWs, the IGCSE Topic Areas and their order, the films and texts, and everything the boys have to learn (every grammar point, vocabulary list and verb set) are as in the September 2026 version, apart from the one change and one emphasis in F Block Michaelmas described below. The details are below.

How long an EW lasts. F Block EWs are meant to last 30 minutes, and E and D Block EWs 40 minutes. These are the maximum time a boy should need to complete an EW.

F Block Michaelmas: the same shape, with one change and one emphasis

The shape of the Half is the same: three Schools and two EWs a week, the same Grammar EWs and the same three Paris Powerpoints. Two things, however, need your attention: each Master now chooses ONLY ONE of either Le Petit Nicolas or Extr@ and keeps to that one throughout the Half, and, as was always intended, Quizlet Verbs should be taken in tiers, never as one long list.

1st School + EW 1: Boys alternate between a) doing a prescribed Grammar EW, either in the traditional way (the PDF recap and the Pre-Test Exercises in OneNote) or in the new way (the recap, checklist and exercises in the app), whichever the Master prefers, and b) doing a Quizlet Verbs EW (on Quizlet as before, provided the Master breaks the list into manageable chunks, or in the app, where the approved chunks are ready to go) where they learn a prescribed set, taken in tiers (Essential first, then Important, then Useful) rather than all 100 verbs at once (in F Mich Week 2, only Essential 1: the first 20 of the 40 Essential verbs), and perhaps complete a short timed test at the end (a link in OneNote). One school a week is devoted to reviewing these tasks. Quizlet Verbs are the SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT part of the Michaelmas course, and this takes top priority.

2nd school + EW 2: Boys alternate between doing something related to EITHER Le Petit Nicolas OR Extr@, never both. One school a week is devoted to reviewing these tasks. To free up time, each Master chooses ONLY ONE of the two for the Half, whichever the Master thinks best suits the Div and their own teaching style (the tables below show both, and the one not chosen is simply dropped). In the Trials, boys then write just one essay, on whichever of the two their Div has studied.

3rd School: this is spent consolidating, catching up and, over the course of the Half as a whole, going through the three Paris Powerpoints in a way that is engaging and fun. The Trial requires only a familiarity with the content of the Powerpoints, and that part is manageable and multiple choice, so this is about the culture: being interesting and enjoyable, not about learning every word on the slides.

F Block after Christmas: the same shape, with IGCSE content

In Lent and Summer, Le Petit Nicolas and Extr@ are replaced with IGCSE content. The Paris Powerpoints are still replaced by Francophonie Powerpoints.

IGCSE Content spread over F, E and D and in the order set out in this scheme

We will be doing the IGCSE TOPIC AREAS (A-E) in order (see the IGCSE checklist at the end of this document). In F Block, we will cover TOPIC AREA A.

Grammar and Vocabulary prescriptions: familiar content, with alternatives

These continue unchanged, as in Michaelmas.

What is new (all of it optional)

How the boys can practise. The apps are an alternative to the existing ways of teaching the material, not a replacement for them, and none of it is compulsory. The PDFs, OneNote and Quizlet all remain available and unchanged: the core material is completely unchanged, and you are welcome to carry on teaching it in your own way.

F Block: Quizlet Verbs (an emphasis). As was always intended, Quizlet Verbs in F should be taken in tiers to suit the level of the Div, never as one long list: the 100 verbs are graded Essential (40), Important (30) and Useful (30). The order is Essential 1 (the first 20), Essential 2 (the second 20), Important (the next 30), then Useful (the last 30). The 40 Essential verbs are themselves split in two, because 40 infinitives is too many for some boys at first: Essential 1 (the first 20) and Essential 2 (the second 20). F Mich Week 2 is Essential 1 only, not all 100 infinitives at once. Essential 2 follows, then Important and Useful in later Quizlet weeks, or as stretch for boys who are ready. Quizlet can still be used for the verbs, provided the Master breaks the lists into manageable chunks; the app already has approved chunks ready to go. The same split works for the tenses: choose Present, say, and then Essential 1, Essential 2 or Essential (both). In the Michaelmas Trials, Part I tests the Quizlet verbs by tier, in Infinitive, Present and Perfect with avoir only (no être or reflexive perfects). There are 40 questions in four sections, scaled to 20 marks: A (Useful verbs) translate a French verb form into English; B (Essential verbs) change the person; C (Important verbs) translate into French with the infinitive given; D (Essential verbs) translate into French with no help. No verb has its infinitive shown in one section and tested in another. So, for the Michaelmas Trials, boys need to have done the infinitives, the Present tense and the Perfect tense of avoir verbs only. By the Summer exam they add, in addition to those, the Perfect tense with être verbs and the Future tense.

E Block: Quizlet Verbs and the Trials. The same three tiers apply in E. In the Michaelmas Trials, Part I has 20 verb questions and 20 core vocabulary questions, scaled to 20 marks. The tenses are Infinitive, Present, Perfect (all verbs, including être and reflexive verbs), Imperfect and Future. Verbs A (Useful verbs) change the person; Verbs B (Important verbs) translate into French with the infinitive given; Verbs C (Essential verbs) translate into French with no help. In the vocabulary, A and B are French into English and then English into French, each with 5 Essential and 5 Important words. The Summer Trials use the same tenses as the E Michaelmas Trials.

Print or online, as you prefer. Grammar and Vocabulary EWs can be set using the printed resources or the online ones in the apps, whichever you prefer. Every recap, list, exercise and test can be viewed and printed, and everything is also there to use on screen. Or, as usual, feel free to make up your own tests of any kind to use instead.

Grammar EWs. Every grammar EW in this scheme now has its own chapter in the Eton IGCSE Vocab and Gr EWs app, named as in the tables below (for example F Mich 3): a new-style recap based on the department pamphlet, a “Now I can…” checklist for the boys to work through, and new exercises written to the same prescription. They are an alternative to the traditional route (the PDF recap and the Pre-Test Exercises in OneNote), which is unchanged and still available: use whichever you prefer. The links in this document go to the new way, because you already know where to find the old. Every exercise is graded Easy, Medium or Hard, so the same grammar point can be set at different levels for different boys in the same Div.

Vocabulary EWs. The weekly lists and their names are unchanged. Boys can learn them in the app (the links in this document go there) or, as before, on the Quizlet sets: use whichever you prefer. Each list (about 90 words) is now graded by how useful a word is to be able to produce in the IGCSE: Essential (about 20), Important (about 25) and Recognition (the rest). The default EW is the Essential and Important words, English → French, which is about 45 words; boys who can are encouraged to go further. Words are also graded Easy, Medium or Hard by how close they look to English.

Quizlet Verbs. The prescribed verb sets are the same. They are now also in the Eton French Verbs app, as an alternative to Quizlet (Quizlet can still be used, provided the Master breaks the lists into manageable chunks), where each verb is graded Essential, Important or Useful, and boys can drill them, take timed tests and view or print the full conjugations. The 100 verbs are graded Essential (40), Important (30) and Useful (30, for recognition only). Essential can be taken as Essential 1 (the first 20) and Essential 2 (the second 20), for the infinitives and for every tense. F Mich Week 2 is Essential 1 only, not all 100 infinitives at once; Essential 2, Important and Useful follow in later Quizlet weeks, or as stretch for boys who are ready.

Differentiation throughout. In the apps everything is graded so that one Div can work at several levels at once. Vocabulary: Essential, Important, Recognition. Verbs: Essential, Important, Useful. Exercises and tests: Easy, Medium, Hard, and Mixed for test day. Boys who need more support stay on Essential and Easy; boys who are ready move up. Nobody is required to do more than the core.

A bank of ready-made tests. For every core vocabulary list and grammar EW, in every block, there is a ready-to-go page in the shared French OneNote, as usual, with a Learn link and an Easy, Medium, Hard and Mixed test. Mixed is the one to use on test day. Any test can be printed at the click of a button and done on paper if you prefer, with no answers on the printout unless you tick the box. Simply copy and paste the link into the OneNote page you plan to distribute to boys, and you are all set up to go.

Your own tests. You are equally free to create your own tests of any kind using traditional methods. Any Master who wants to can also (entirely optionally) set a timed Spot Test to their own specification in the apps, create a link and share it with their Divs through OneNote or Teams. It can be reused from year to year or filed away in your own system. The boys do it on the iPad with the Apple Pencil, mark it themselves and hand it in as a PDF.

Where to find out more. The colleague quick-start guide explains all of this on one page.

F Block

F Block · Michaelmas Half

CHOOSE ONE ONLY: Le Petit Nicolas OR Extr@

Each Master teaches EITHER Le Petit Nicolas OR Extr@ this Half, never both, whichever best suits the Div and their own teaching style. Where the middle column lists both, follow only the one you have chosen. In the Trials, boys write one essay, on whichever of the two their Div has studied.

Why: the F Block course, above all in the Michaelmas Half, is meant to be a time for consolidating the basics of French: how verbs are conjugated and how the grammar works. Many boys arrive from Common Entrance unable to conjugate a verb and with little awareness of grammar. Teaching only one of the two means less content to cover and frees up real time for that work.

Four EWs a week

In both C Block and B Block the boys have four EWs a week.

Language-based translation practice, for the translation paper.

What stays the same

Boys will continue to have a Grammar EW on a regular basis (alternating with the topic EWs) and vocabulary work every week (formerly VV work, now known as TVE work), albeit with new Quizlet sets. They will continue to have two French Masters, one presiding over the language and topics, and the other covering the films and literary texts and also doing weekly translation work: language work unrelated to the set film or text and geared towards the translation paper. We will continue to do twice as many films and texts as is required. Boys will continue to do weekly one to one oral sessions with the assistant. There continues to be a personal researched topics for boys in the oral, too.

How long an EW lasts. Specialist EWs are meant to last 60 minutes. This is the maximum time a boy should need to complete an EW.

The Edexcel course (as before)

There are four themes, and the fourth theme (The French Occupation) ties in perfectly with our text and film.

There is some translation into English (as well as translation from English into French).

We continue to have access to our shared Edexcel OneNote, with a huge array of different resources for teaching the topics that suit a range of teaching styles. You can use whichever resources you want to teach the topics in an effective way.

The essential thing to remember is that whether we are teaching a text, a film, or a theme, the number one priority should be the quality of the boys' French language. This should be reflected in the teaching of both the language and cultural Master.

What is new (all of it optional)

How the boys can practise. The apps are an alternative to the existing ways of teaching the material, not a replacement for them, and none of it is compulsory. The PDFs and OneNote remain available and unchanged: the core material is completely unchanged, and you are welcome to carry on teaching it in your own way.

Print or online, as you prefer. Grammar and Vocabulary EWs can be set using the printed resources or the online ones in the apps, whichever you prefer. Every recap, list, exercise and test can be viewed and printed, and everything is also there to use on screen. Or, as usual, feel free to make up your own tests of any kind to use instead.

Grammar EWs. The Grammar EWs are the same, with the same numbers (EW Grammar 1 to 14 for C Block and 15 to 20 for B Block). The traditional route (PDF and OneNote) is unchanged. As an alternative, each also has a chapter in the Eton A Level app: a new-style recap, a “Now I can…” checklist and new exercises, graded Easy, Medium or Hard.

VV work. Each weekly set is now split into the topic vocabulary (TV) and the verb structures (VS), and the structures are graded Essential (the five most important, to be written in French), Important (the next five) and Recognition (all the others, to be understood). The default for every boy is the topic vocabulary plus the five Essential structures, known for short as TVE (TVE1a, TVE1b and so on). That is the minimum each boy learns, it cuts the workload by about 40% compared with the whole set, and it is what tests in School should be based on. Boys who can are encouraged to add the Important structures and some of the Recognition ones. In the tables below, the vocabulary column shows the weekly TVE set: it is the default for every week, so nothing needs to be set.

A bank of ready-made tests. For every core TVE set and grammar EW, in both blocks, there is a ready-to-go page in the shared French OneNote, as usual, with a Learn link and an Easy, Medium, Hard and Mixed test. Mixed is the one to use on test day. Any test can be printed at the click of a button and done on paper if you prefer, with no answers on the printout unless you tick the box. Simply copy and paste the link into the OneNote page you plan to distribute to boys, and you are all set up to go.

Your own tests. You are equally free to create your own tests of any kind using traditional methods. Any Master who wants to can also (entirely optionally) set a timed Spot Test to their own specification in the apps, create a link and share it with their Divs through OneNote or Teams. It can be reused from year to year or filed away in your own system.
