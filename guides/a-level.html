Part 1. Read this first: how every Eton app works

All of the Eton apps share the same look, the same buttons and the same keys. Learn them once here and you will know them everywhere. Later parts describe each app on its own, grouped by level and then by language.

1.1 Getting into an app

  • Each app is one web page. Your teacher gives you the link (usually in OneNote or Teams). Open it in your browser on your iPad or computer.

  • Each page name starts with Eton. The exact page name and link are printed at the top of every app's entry in this guide.

  • If your teacher publishes a newer version, the page refreshes itself once. Your saved answers are kept.

  • Tests that your teacher sets you need an internet connection. Pages that only drill are happy once they have loaded.

  • Tap 🔊 Voices (top of every app) to check which voice reads each language. On an iPad the voice sounds far better if you download it first: Settings > Accessibility > Spoken Content > Voices.

  • Tap ⌂ Home to go back to the start screen. It stops any audio.

  • Tap ← Back (next to Home, once you have left the first screen) to go back exactly one step, for example from a drill to its set-up screen, or from a set-up screen to the menu. It also stops any audio.

1.3 The self-marking drill

This is the heart of every app. You mark yourself, so be honest. Nobody can see your score and nobody is fooled by an inflated one.

  1. Press ▶ Start Drill. A card appears and the question is read aloud. A timer bar shrinks.

  2. Say the answer out loud (or write it). Do this before you look.

  3. Press Show Answer, or press →. The answer appears and is read aloud. Press 🔊 Replay to hear it again. If the timer runs out the answer appears by itself ("Time's up!").

  4. Mark yourself with the three buttons or the arrow keys (table below).

  5. At the end you get your score. Cards you marked as major errors come back for a second go.

Mark Key What happens
✗ Major error ← You did not know it, or it was badly wrong. The card is saved on your error list and comes back in the Major Error Drill.
~ Nearly ↓ Close, with a small slip (an accent, an ending). The card comes back 2 or 3 cards later in the same drill.
✓ Got it → Right. It counts as a point.

Showing the answer: press → (in most apps Space or Enter also work). Then ←, ↓ and → mark the card.

  • The bar at the top shows your running score (✓, ~ and ✗ counts).

  • ▶ Errors opens your error list so far.

  • ⚙ Settings (where you see it) pauses the drill so you can change the direction or the number of cards. Press ▶ Apply & resume to carry on. Settings also lets you change Cards in this drill (the cards you have done stay; the rest are topped up or trimmed) and the Time per card.

  • In a verb Drill the infinitive is hidden, so you practise the conjugation without leaning on it. The 💡 Infinitive button in the top bar switches it On or Off at any moment, even in the middle of a card.

  • In a verb Drill, ⚙ Settings in the top bar pauses the card so you can change the Time per card, switch the infinitive on or off, or change Cards in this drill. Press ▶ Apply & resume to carry on.

  • In the Major Error Drill you can also press ⏭ Ignore to skip a card with no penalty.

The end screen

  • Your score as x / total and a percentage.

  • Either "No major errors: clean sweep" or "N cards to revisit". Copy list copies those cards so you can paste them into OneNote.

  • ▶ Drill major errors again, ↻ Do same set again (all the cards of your selection, not just the first few) and ⌂ Home.

Tip Use Nearly honestly. It is the button that brings a card back quickly, and that repetition is what makes the card stick.

1.4 Leisure Mode (☕)

Leisure Mode is for soaking up the language. Make your selection, press ☕ Leisure Mode and sit back. Each card is read aloud, there is a pause for you to answer in your head or out loud, then the answer is shown and read, and the next card follows. It loops for ever, reshuffling. There is no marking, no score and no error list.

Tap ⚙ Settings while it plays to pause it and change the direction, the mix or the thinking time; ▶ Apply & resume carries on from the same card.

  • Thinking time: starts at 4 seconds. The − and + buttons speed it up or slow it down while it plays; the change applies from the next pause.

  • ⏸ Pause / ▶ Resume: or press Space.

  • 🔊 Replay repeats the current card. ⌂ Home stops.

Tip Leisure Mode is excellent in the evening, on a walk or on a journey. Try answering out loud before the answer arrives.

1.5 ⚡ Eton Speed Challenge (the tile game)

A fast game for pairs or teams. It fills the screen with numbered tiles. Each tile shows a prompt (usually English).

In the grammar version every tile says what to do: a gold ✏️ Translate into French label on a translation, and a purple 📘 Give the rule label on a rule question.

The bar under the tiles is in three groups: New set, Pause timer, Reveal all and How to play first; the round settings (Level, Tiles, Time, Teams and so on) in the middle, under a This round label; and Print sheet, Sound on and the test buttons last, under More tools.

  1. Choose the settings: Level (Easy / Medium / Hard / Mixed), Tiles (6, 9, 12 or 16), Time, Teams (Off, 2, 3 or 4; click a team name to rename it), Each turn (20 seconds to 1 minute) and Direction. If your list has fewer items than the number of tiles, a short note says so and you get one tile for each item.

  2. Say the number of a tile and then say the answer aloud.

  3. Click the tile (or type its number) to reveal and hear the correct answer.

  4. Press ✓ Right (the tile greys out and you score a point) or ✗ Wrong (a new item replaces it).

  5. Beeps sound at one third and two thirds of the time. A buzzer sounds at the end.

  6. In team games, a wrong answer passes the turn to the next team at once, and a flashing sign shows whose go it is. The team with the most tiles wins.

When the game ends you can ▶ Play the N left on screen, ✗ Play the N that were wrong, 📋 Go through the wrong ones (no timer) or deal a 🔀 New full set.

Other buttons: ❓ How to play, 🔀 New set, ⏸ Pause timer, 👁 Reveal all, 🖨 Print sheet, 🔊 Sound on. The game also has its own No-limit test and Spot Test buttons that use the tiles you were playing with.

A Speed session is the same game but set by your teacher through a link. It is locked to your teacher's choice, starts with 2 teams, 16 tiles, English to the foreign language, 20 seconds a turn and a 3 minute game, and you stay in it until your teacher says stop. There is no leaving it.

1.5a ★ My personalised set, ✍ Spell and 🧩 Match (VVs Drill)

These are on the VVs Drill set-up screen only.

  • ★ Personalised set. Every card you mark ✗ Major error in a drill joins your personalised set automatically. ~ Nearly just brings the card back a few cards later: if it still beats you, mark it ✗. Under FIRST · What do you want to practise? tap ★ My personalised set, then choose Say it, Spell it, Match it, ⚡ Speed Challenge, Leisure Mode or Test exactly as you would on any other set. Once it is chosen you can narrow it to Hard only, Tricky + hard or Due today.

  • ? How does this work? sits beside ★ My personalised set and gives a short explanation in plain words.

  • ✎ Edit lists your cards. Tap the level (Hard, Tricky, Easier) to change it, tap ✕ to take a card out, or choose any set and press + Add cards from this set to add cards yourself.

  • Due today. A card you missed comes back after 1 day, then 3 days, then 7 days. Get it right each time and it stops coming back.

  • 🔗 Save my personalised set gives you a link. Press Copy link and paste it into your own OneNote, under a clear title, and keep that page tidy. Tap that link on any device and the app opens with your set ready. Your set lives only on this iPad, in this browser. If the browser data is cleared, or you use another device, it is gone. The link is the only safe copy, and looking after it is up to you. Paste a fresh link after each big session.

  • ✍ Spell. The app says the French aloud. Type what you hear, or write it in the box with your Apple Pencil (iPadOS turns your handwriting into text; if nothing happens, check that Settings, Apple Pencil, Scribble is on), the sentence checks itself when the last tile goes in; if it is wrong, move the tiles round. Clue: put one tile in for me places one correct tile in a random empty place as an extra clue. Another sentence swaps a sentence you do not like for a new one. Words in green are right, wavy orange is an accent slip, red is wrong or missing. Accents count. Then mark yourself with the usual ✗ Major error ← · ~ Nearly ↓ · ✓ Got it → row. Use 🔊 Listen to hear it again and Show English if you need to check you have understood it — useful for a longer sentence, where it is really a short translation into French as much as a spelling exercise. It uses whichever set you have chosen, and the number of cards you picked under Cards per session.

  • Speed Challenge and the personalised set. The Speed Challenge works on whole lists, so it is not offered for ★ My personalised set.

  • 🧩 Match. Ten pairs. Tap an English card, then its French. A wrong pair adds 3 seconds. The app shows your time and your best time on this iPad. It is a good five-minute warm-up for a Div. Match opens with a two-second picture splash with a quick arcade power-up sound (the 🔊 button turns it off), and the same picture glows faintly behind the game. Each matched pair turns its own colour and makes a soft pop, a wrong pair shakes and adds the 3 seconds, and the timer turns orange and then red as it climbs. Each game opens with a different picture across the top, and a little ghost 👻 races along a bar at your best time, so you can see whether you are ahead of it. Matching several pairs in a row builds a streak, you earn up to three stars at the end, and a new best time gets a celebration. The pairs are not read aloud unless you tap 🗣 Voice off to switch the voice on. The 🔊 button turns the sound effects off.

1.7 The timed “Spot Test” with the Apple Pencil, step by step

  1. Press the big yellow ✍ Do it with Apple Pencil button. A box asks you to Choose your level. Leave it on Mixed unless your teacher has told you otherwise. Your level is printed at the top of your PDF.

  2. Choose ⏱ Timed (use this in Birley Schools, or when your teacher has set the test as an EW) or 🏠 Untimed. You cannot change your mind once you start. The clock starts the moment you tap. It can be paused only for a real emergency (a fire alarm, for example): press ⏸ Pause at the top right, type (or write with your Apple Pencil) why you are pausing, then press ▶ Resume. While paused the clock is stopped and the test is hidden, you must still stay on the page (leaving it still sounds the error buzz and is logged), and every pause is printed on your PDF. While a timed test is open your iPad stays awake (it will not dim or lock itself); if you lock it yourself with the side button, that is logged like leaving the page. If the clock at the top of the screen distracts you, tap it to make it smaller (tap again to make it bigger); it stays on screen. Blank answers score 0. No timed test runs longer than 4½ minutes: a test of short answers (words or verb forms) can have up to 20 items, while longer answers give fewer.

  3. Answer each item. Each has two tabs: ✍️ Handwrite (Apple Pencil, black ink) and ⌨️ Type. Only type if you type in exams or have no Pencil.

  4. When finished, press 🔒 I've attempted all N: lock in. In a timed test you get 30 seconds of grace after the time ends, and then your answers lock themselves.

  5. Press ✔ Show answers & mark. Correct your own answer in red first (tick it if perfect, or underline the errors). You cannot choose a mark until there is red on the answer. Once you have used red pen, the app reminds you in red to tap a mark. If those red reminders are too much, press 🔕 Turn off the red “tap your mark” alerts (it appears the first time, or in the marking box); press 🔔 Turn the red alerts back on to bring them back. Your choice is remembered on that iPad. Without the alerts, an unmarked answer still scores 0.

  6. Now give yourself a mark. Single words and forms: 2 Right, 1 Nearly (a missing accent, a spelling slip, a wrong ending or gender), 0 Wrong. Sentences and structures: 4 Perfect, 3 Mostly right, 2 About half right, 1 Only a word or two right, 0 Nothing right. The test is marked out of 20. In a timed test the marking time is limited too, and anything you have not marked scores 0. If you finish marking early, tap I've finished marking: after an Are you sure? check, the marking timer stops and you go straight on to What I learned.

  7. Write in the 📝 What I learned box. This is the most important part: what caught you out, and how will you remember it? In Birley Schools you get a couple of minutes; in your House take as long as you need. Try to find 10 things to write (5 is the minimum), numbered 1 to 10: even a single piece of vocabulary or a rule counts. The PDF button unlocks only after you have written for 1 minute.

  8. Press 📄 Save my work as PDF and file it (see 1.9).

Careful Do not leave the page during a test. If you switch to another app or tab, use Split View, reload or press Back, an error buzz sounds and a red warning flashes. The attempt is logged and recorded, and the PDF says how many times you left and for how long.

If you are not sure about a mark, do not stop to ask your teacher. Mark it wrong if in doubt and ask afterwards.

  • Your attempt number is shown ("ATTEMPT n ON THIS TEST").

  • If a page reloads by accident, reopening the same test within 12 hours picks up where you were. There is a Start afresh option.

  • When you have finished, marked and saved your PDF, you can press 🔄 Take a fresh test at the bottom of the page for new questions with the same settings. The test you have just done is cleared, so save its PDF first. Teacher-set tests do not have this button.

1.8 Writing with the Apple Pencil (Eton PEN)

  • Written answers have two tabs: ✍️ Handwrite (the default) and ⌨️ Type.

  • Pen tools: Pen, Thin / Medium, Eraser, Undo, Clear and + space (adds more room).

  • When the app detects your Pencil, your finger no longer draws: it scrolls the page.

  • Write in black ink. Once marking begins the black ink locks, and a 🖊 Red pen: corrections appears. All corrections go in red. The eraser then removes red only.

  • Typed answers get a red "Corrections" box instead.

1.9 Saving your work to OneNote

Marked papers and timed tests end with 📄 Save my work as PDF (or 📄 Save marked paper as PDF).

  1. The app asks for your name once and remembers it. It also gives you a OneNote page title (date, then a clear title such as "Spot Test on VV10a") with a Copy button.

  2. On an iPad the print screen opens. Do not print. Tap the Share button, then OneNote.

  3. Choose your Div's Class OneNote, then your name, then Class Work (work done in Birley Schools) or EW (work done in your House).

  4. Paste the title you copied, then press Send.

  5. In OneNote, tap the PDF icon and choose Printout so that the pages show in full. (The paper-clip File button only adds a small attachment box.)

On a computer: Save as PDF, then in OneNote choose Insert > File Printout. If the share route fails on an iPad, use Save to Files, then in OneNote Insert > PDF.

1.10 Grammar recaps and "Now I can" checklists

Every grammar chapter in the Gr EWs apps has an Eton grammar recap: a clear explanation of the rules, with examples. Read the recap before you drill the chapter. All recaps use the same colours, and a colour key sits at the top of each one:

Colour What it means
Core rule (blue) The rule itself.
Examples & how to say (green) Examples, with how to say them.
Tips & patterns (amber) Tips and patterns that help you remember.
Exceptions: learn by heart (red) The awkward ones. Learn them.
Watch out (yellow) The points to be careful about.
Lists & vocab (white) Lists and vocabulary to learn.

Each recap also has two special boxes:

  • Soon I will be able to… (grey dashed box, near the top): what you are about to learn. It is for reading, and cannot be ticked.

  • Now I can… (bright yellow box at the very end): a checklist for after you have drilled the chapter. Tick each box when you can do it, then press the red Check button to compare with a model answer (the button then says Hide). The prompts are in English. The French or Italian appears only in the model answer.

Your ticks are saved on that device and in that browser only. They do not go to your teacher, they do not show on your other devices, and clearing the site data wipes them. They do not print either. You find the recaps in the A Level app (section 2.1, VVs and Gr EWs).

Links from your teacher. If your teacher pastes a link to a grammar recap (for example into OneNote), tap it and the recap for the right chapter or chapters opens straight away, with the live Now I can… boxes and their Check buttons. At the top of that page is a Practise this grammar bar with Start Drill, Test, Spot Test and Speed Challenge buttons, already set up for those chapters or sets. A link to a vocab list works the same way: tap it and the app opens with exactly that list chosen, ready to view, drill, test yourself or play Speed.

Pick several recaps at once. Under the chapter drop-down, press "Grammar recaps: pick one or several". Search for a topic, Tick all, or tick any combination of Gr EWs chapters and Grammar Drills sets, then press Read ↓ or 🖨 Print / Save as PDF. The options are Each recap on a new page, Larger text, Include "Soon I will…" and "Now I can…" and Print the model answers too. To go through only your checklists, switch on Only the "Now I can…" tick boxes: the page then shows just the tick-box lists of the chapters you picked, one after another, and you can print that too.

Tip Be strict. Tick a box only if you could do it from memory, then press Check to see whether you really could.

1.11 Things to ignore

Every app contains tools that belong to your teacher. You will never need them, and you should leave them alone:

  • The Eton crest in the header (tapping it just says "Not available").

  • Any button marked ✎ Edit, ✏️ Edit Vocab, Print Content, Study Sheet, ⬇ Export HTML or 👩‍🏫 Teacher.

  • Any button starting 🔑 (making test links). You will not even see these unless your teacher has set up a key on that device.

VVs Drill

  • What to practise: four labelled rows. A navy and gold button means "this is the row I am using". Options inside a row show on a pale gold background when chosen, and the ones you can tick also show a ✓. The padlock has its own colours: blue when locked (French to English only) and green when unlocked (either way round). Topic Vocab Only (TV1a, TV1b, TV1c, TV1 Entire up to TV12 Entire) is the topic vocabulary only; it has a glowing green border, so it is easy to find if you want the topic vocabulary on its own. Verb Structures Only (VS1a up to VS12 Entire) is the verb structures only, in alphabetical order. Vocab and Verbs together (Michaelmas Trials, Summer Trials, then VV1a up to VV12 Entire) is both. Topic Vocab + chosen structures, the top row inside the red box, is one week of topic vocabulary plus whichever of its verb structures you choose (see below); it is where most pupils start. The actual week is not chosen on these rows: press a row's button to settle its own structure buttons (Essential, Important, Recognition and so on), then press the single 📖 Choose a set button below the four rows. This opens a full-screen list of every week from all four rows, grouped under the same four headings, with a search box at the top (for example type "3b" to jump straight to it). Tap any week in the list and it is chosen immediately and the list closes; the button then reads 📖 Change set with the week's name underneath, and the number of phrases is shown under the rows. TV1 to TV12, VS1 to VS12 and VV1 to VV12 all line up with Topics 1 to 12.

  • Which structures: five buttons under Verb Structures Only choose how many of the verb structures to practise. Essential are the five most important structures of each week: learn them and be able to write them in French. Important are the next five. Essential + Important gives you both together, ten a week. Recognition are all the others: you only need to understand them, so the drill sets itself to French to English and Swap will not turn it round. A teacher or a stronger pupil can press the 🔒 French → English only button that appears under the choices to unlock it and practise Recognition either way round; press it again to lock. All structures is every verb structure of the week, as before. The sets are named to match, for example VS Essential 3b or VS Recognition 3b, and 📖 View & Print and the ⚡ Speed Challenge list them too. The same structure can appear in more than one week; it counts as Essential or Important only in the week where it is marked.

  • Topic Vocab + chosen structures: this is the top block, with a small note saying it is the usual choice for the weekly learning EW. Nothing is chosen when the page opens, and tapping a button such as Topic Vocab + chosen structures or Important does not choose a week for you: press 📖 Choose a set first to pick a week yourself. What to practise is the first thing on the screen; Direction and Mix both ways are tucked into the ⚙ Settings bar (tap Change). Until you do, all six picture tiles (Say it, Spell it, Match it, ⚡ Speed Challenge, Leisure Mode and Test) stay greyed out. It starts as that week's topic vocabulary plus its Essential structures, which is what most pupils need. For short this default is called TVE (topic vocabulary plus the five Essential structures), so a week's default set is TVE1a, TVE1b, TVE1c, TVE2a and so on up to TVE12c. Press Important to add those too, and Recognition to add those as well. You can have any mix, but at least one must stay on. With Essential and Important only, everything is practised the way round you choose under Direction (English to French to begin with). The padlock appears next to Recognition only when you tick Recognition, and it comes on by itself. The sets are named to match, for example TV + Essential 3b or TV + Essential + Recognition 3b. The 🔒 Recognition: French → English only padlock locks only the Recognition phrases to French to English, in the drill, Leisure Mode and tests. Every other phrase (the topic vocabulary and the Essential and Important structures) is still practised the way round you choose, English to French to begin with. If a test mixes the two, each question shows a flag for its direction. If you want to be tested on the Recognition vocabulary into French as well as into English, simply press the padlock to unlock it (🔓 Recognition: either direction); a note under the buttons tells you this. If you press the padlock yourself, it remembers that choice on this device until you next add or remove Recognition. 📖 View & Print and the ⚡ Speed Challenge list these sets too.

  • TV1a is the topic vocabulary of VV1a and VS1a is its verb structures, so TV1a and VS1a together make VV1a (the same goes for every set). Your teacher will tell you which sets to learn. 📖 View & Print and the ⚡ Speed Challenge list the same groups.

  • NEXT · How do you want to practise it? Once a set is chosen, tap one of the picture tiles: Say it, ✍ Spell it, 🧩 Match it, 🧱 Build it (tap or drag the word tiles into the right order to rebuild a full sentence, then press Check; the English only appears if you press Give me a clue), ⚡ Speed Challenge, ☕ Leisure Mode or 📝 Test (the answers stay hidden until you press Show answers). The ⚙ Settings bar (tap Change) holds Cards per session, Timer per card, ⏱ Autotime, Direction with ⇄ Swap, and Mix both ways (Off, 50–50 or 70–30, so some cards go into French and some back into English). 📖 View & Print is under the tiles. ★ My personalised set, ✍ Spell it and 🧩 Match it are described in 1.5a.

  • Keys here: arrows only (→ shows, then ← ↓ → mark). Space and Enter do not work in this drill.

  • View & Print: an item range, a search box ("Search English or French…"), Tick all / Untick all, Show both / Hide French / Hide English, and print buttons 🖨 List, 🖨 Study Sheet, 📝 Test → French, 📝 Test → English (and the two Mix both ways tests). The tests print without the answers, so the sheet can go straight to the boys; tick Include the answers on a separate last page above the print buttons if you want the answer key as well. Group by (Set, Topic, Tier or One list) and Sort (Original order or A–Z) decide how the list is arranged on screen and on every printout, so you can print all the verb structures together, grouped by Essential, Important and Recognition. A Select all button on each group heading ticks every set in it, and a small E, I or R beside a verb structure shows its tier.

Info cards: where an English explanation sits between asterisks, do not translate it. Give the French thing it refers to.

Translation Practice

  • 122 passages. Real past papers: 9 into French and 8 into English. The rest are AI passages.

  • Choose Direction (Into French or Into English), Papers (Real Past Papers or Include AI Generated), Year (or, with AI included, a Topic from 1 to 12). Go to ref takes a code such as FR023. 🎲 Random picks one.

  1. Write your translation first, no exceptions. Use the Pencil pad. The ⏱ Timer can be OFF or ON (5, 8, 10, 15, 20 or 30 minutes; the exam allows 30). At zero the mark scheme appears by itself.

  2. Press ▶ Reveal Mark Scheme (or ⏹ Stop Timer & Reveal). The pen locks. Correct in red.

  3. The passage is split into 20 chunks. Tap a chunk to open it. You see the model answer, "Also accepted" and examiner notes. Mark each chunk ✓ Got it or ✗ Major error (you can Change mark). Reveal All opens everything.

  4. Your Score: n / 20 shows. Press ▶ See Results, then 📄 Save marked paper as PDF.

Grammar Drills

  • 12 sets of 88 sentences: The Passive Voice, Past Participle Agreement, The Subjunctive, SI Clauses, Object Pronouns, Relative Pronouns, Imparfait vs Passé composé, Negative Structures, Gerund / EN FAISANT, DEPUIS + present / VENIR DE, RENDRE X ADJ / FAIRE INF X, Infinitive Constructions.

  • Translate only the bold words: each English sentence has the grammar point in bold (the relative pronoun, the subjunctive verb and so on), tagged TRANSLATE ONLY THE BOLD. Write only those words in French. When you press Show Answer the whole French sentence appears, with just that part highlighted. This works the same way in drills, printed tests, Spot Tests (including timed) and the Eton Speed Challenge. Because you are writing only a word or two, these count as short answers in a timed test, so a test has 4 per box (20 items) instead of 10.

  • Each set has 40 generic sentences and 4 for each topic. Filter by topic chips: All, Generic, Family, Education, Work, Music, Media, Traditions, Immigration, Integration, Far Right, Occupation, Vichy, Resistance.

  • Tap a set to open it, or tick several sets and press ▶ Start Drill: selected sets. On a set screen, and again above the question card during a drill, the bar "Want an Eton grammar recap?" opens the recap for that set. Timer chips are 10, 15, 20 and 30 seconds. The prompt is English to French.

Gr EWs: Grammar Studio (a step-by-step way to learn a chapter)

  • Grammar Studio (all 26 Gr EWs chapters). In Gr EWs, choose just one chapter and the Grammar Studio tile is the first card under How do you want to practise it?, with Grammar Recap beside it (it opens the recap of the ticked chapters straight away) and Drill, Speed Challenge, Spell, Match and Test underneath. It teaches the chapter step by step, one idea at a time, with a quick check after each and a one-line reason. It is built for you to finish in about 45 to 50 minutes, which leaves room for a spot test in your EW slot. It spends its time on what is new at A Level and goes quickly over what you already know from IGCSE. At the very start you can tap the Focus Point you want to begin with, and after each one you choose where to go next.

  • Exhibits. Many screens have a glowing blue button: press it and watch what happens to the French (a word moves, an ending changes, a tense changes), then press the green button to put it back and read the rule again. Only the letters that change are coloured. Where a word has to go somewhere in a sentence, drag the blue word to the gold gap (or tap the gap).

  • Your route. At the start of each Focus Point you can tick the numbered parts you want taught and press Teach me just the parts I have ticked, or choose Teach me all of this or I know all of this: test me. Test me skips the teaching and asks 4 questions on that part, the last one the hardest. Get them right and you move on; get the hardest one wrong, or two wrong, and the Studio teaches you the whole part, one screen at a time, and you must use each interactive exhibit before you can carry on. The screen warns you of this before you choose, so choose test me only if you really do know the part. Not sure? Reteach me this bit takes you to the right lesson and back. A wrong answer is fine: tap the right one to carry on, and the question comes back at the end of the Focus Point. After each Focus Point, tick I can now confidently… only when you honestly can, or go back and review.

  • Need more help? opens the Eton grammar recap at the exact section you need, with a ← Back to the Grammar Studio (where I was) button at the top and at that section to bring you straight back.

  • Top bar and progress sheet. The top bar has ← Back, a small timer, Pause, My progress sheet and ✕ Exit. My progress sheet asks for your name and shows your active time, how far you got, your route and your results, with a big red thoroughness circle from 0 to 100. That circle is not a test mark: it shows how fully you worked through the chapter. Save as PDF turns it into a PDF to put in OneNote.

Gr EWs

  • 26 chapters (Gr 1 Present and Imperfect, through Gr 15 Subjunctive, Gr 19 Adjectives, to Gr 20 Adverbs and faire + infinitive, then six chapters for the appendices at the back of the handout: Gr 21 Whoever, however, whatever; Gr 22 The past historic and the imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive (these two only to recognise); Gr 23 to Gr 25 Prepositional usage (About to Below, From to Out of, Over to With); Gr 26 Time, place and verb structures). About 4,800 items. The recaps for Gr 21 to Gr 26 carry every entry from the handout, with the English. Each chapter has plenty of translation sentences, so tests repeat much less. Many of the sentences, and some of the examples in the recaps, use words from the topic vocabulary of the weeks the chapter is set (the topic words and the Essential structures). They are there to practise the grammar: any word you might not know is given in the question.

  • Level (Easy / Medium / Hard / Mixed): every Gr EWs translation has been rated on its own for how hard the grammar is to get right: the trickiest thing in that sentence (an exception, an agreement, a pronoun order, a tense or mood choice). Sentence length and how much the words look like English do not decide the level. Hard means the sentence really is hard, so the early chapters have few Hard sentences and the later chapters have many. If a chapter has few or none at the level you pick, the test tells you how many it could use.

  • Two item types: 📖 Grammar Rule and ✏️ Translate. The prompt is always English and the answer French. A rule card is spoken as "Point de grammaire".

  • Test me on: All, 📘 Rules only or ✏️ Translations only. This affects the drill and the tests.

  • Combine chapters: tick two or more, with Select all and Clear. The buttons then say "Gr 1 + Gr 3" and "(combo)".

  • Grammar recap: under the Chapter drop-down is the bar "Want an Eton grammar recap? Gr N · [chapter title]". Click it to open the recap for the chapter you chose (it is not shown inside the drill). The bar opens one chapter. To read, print or tick through several chapters, use Grammar recaps: pick one or several (see 1.10). The headings follow the numbered Points on your EW handouts, for example "Point 7 · The 50 completely irregular presents".

  • Soon I will… and Now I can…: every Gr EWs chapter has 12 of each (see 1.10 for the colour key and the checklist). The 12 Grammar Drills sets have 4 to 10 "Now I can" statements each.

  • Eton mnemonics in the recaps include ANPQ (the exceptions that go after the past participle: Aucun, Nulle part, Personne, Que; rien stays before it), PJRP (the order of the second parts when there are several: Plus, Jamais, Rien, Personne) and NEG 1 / NEG 2 (ne, and the second part of the negative).

  • The drill card shows a reference such as "Gr 3 [17]". There is no per-card timer. Keys: → ↓ ← and Space.

Eton Metalanguage Formulae

  • 191 grammar-pattern formulae in English, French and Italian. Example: "I have been VERBING for (TIME)" gives "Je VERBE depuis (TIME)".

  • Choose the From and To languages with the flag chips, cards (10, 20, All or a number), timer (10, 15, 20, 30 seconds), then ▶ Start Drill, ☕ Leisure Mode and the test buttons.

Tip Start with a Trials or VVn Entire set before the small a, b and c lists.

Use Rules only in Gr EWs for pure rule recall. Use the Sentence Drill for context and Grammar Drills for sentence-level work.

Part 3. Getting the most from the apps

A simple weekly routine

  1. Learn the material for your set (vocab list, VV set, Gr EW chapter or Approved Material topic) with Read & listen, View & Print or Leisure Mode.

  2. Say it it out loud: say the answer before you press Show. Mark honestly.

  3. Re-drill your errors with ▶ Drill major errors again.

  4. Test yourself with ✍ Spot Test. Write your What I learned notes properly.

  5. File the PDF in OneNote, in the right folder, with the title copied from the app.
