Sharpen your French and Italian

A short guide to a set of free practice apps written for French, Italian and Spanish teaching. Every blue link below opens the right app. No login, nothing to install, and it works on a laptop or a phone.

The idea in one minute

Each app is a fast, self-marked drill. A card appears and is read aloud. You say the answer, press Show Answer, and press one of three keys: ← major error, ↓ nearly, → got it. Cards you miss come back until you know them. A drill of 20 cards takes about five minutes.

The point is finding the gaps. Even strong students find holes in the things that were never drilled: vocabulary and genders, the exact irregular forms, the small grammar points. Be strict with the marking and the gaps show up quickly.

Careful Do not be fooled by the word "IGCSE". Many of these apps carry that label, and it is easy to think "that will be too easy for me". It is not. They contain a great deal that you do not know well enough to dismiss: gender and plural of ordinary nouns, small words and prepositions, the exact irregular forms of verbs, and the grammar points that never quite stuck. Take the label as a description of the exam board, not of how hard the drill will feel. Try a drill before you decide.

The 60-second test: no peeking

Italian French
Passato remoto, io: bere, sapere, volere, rimanere, dire Subjonctif: que je ___ (valoir), (craindre), (acquérir), (cueillir)
Translate: "If it was up to me, I would choose to spend the summer holidays in Sicily." (up to me = dipendere da) Futur, je: cueillir, acquérir, mourir
Translate: "The more I think about it, the more I find that history is much more interesting than geography." Say aloud in French: however rich you are, and no sooner had they left than the baby started crying

Part 1. Italian

Careful Do not be fooled by the word "IGCSE". Many of these apps carry that label, and it is easy to think "that will be too easy for me". It is not. They contain a great deal that you do not know well enough to dismiss: gender and plural of ordinary nouns, small words and prepositions, the exact irregular forms of verbs, and the grammar points that never quite stuck. Take the label as a description of the exam board, not of how hard the drill will feel. Try a drill before you decide.

Italian is where the apps can help you most. There are five good ways in. Start with the first two.

1.1 Verbs: the irregular forms you can never quite hold

▶ Open Eton Italian Verbs

https://apowles.github.io/AP/Eton%20Italian%20Verbs.html

  • 115 IGCSE verbs, with 11 tenses for most. The drill offers Present, Future, Perfect, Imperfect, Conditional, Pres. Subj., Imp. Subj., Past Hist. (passato remoto) and Infinitive. Pluperfect, Future perfect and Conditional perfect appear in the tests.

  • Rarer irregulars are all there: bere (bevvi, berrò, beva, bevessi), rimanere, rompersi, venire, volere, dovere, scegliere, spegnere, togliersi, costringere.

  • Set Regularity to Irregular. Use Full (6 forms) to say a whole tense, or Single form for speed.

  • 🧩 Eton Advanced Verb Structures has about 585 set phrases conjugated in every tense, such as "assestare un duro colpo a X", "andare in estasi per X" and "per quanto ricco tu sia".

  • Use 💬 Sentences for gap-fill sentences with the verb in context.

Why it helps: You see the verb, the person and the tense, and you have to produce the form from memory, which is exactly what a university test does.

1.2 Grammar: 15 chapters, from articles to the subjunctive

▶ Open Eton IGCSE Vocab and Gr EWs

https://apowles.github.io/AP/Eton%20IGCSE%20Vocab%20and%20Gr%20EWs.html

  • Open the page, press 📘 Gr EWs, then 🇮🇹 Italian (Gr EWs always opens in French). There are 750 items: rules, translations and transformations. Do not skip the early chapters: 1 to 10 hold the points that cost marks quietly.

  • Chapters 11 to 15 are the most demanding: 11 combined pronouns and the imperative, 12 relative pronouns and the pluperfect, 13 present and perfect subjunctive, 14 imperfect subjunctive and se + conditional, 15 the passive, future perfect, conditional perfect and ecco + pronouns.

  • Chapters 1 to 10 cover articles, plurals and irregular nouns, articulated prepositions, partitives, ne and ci, piacere, avere or essere, participle agreement, comparatives and negatives.

  • Step ①: in the Choose your chapters box (it has a glowing red line round it, so you can see where to start), tick the chapters you want, as many as you like. Tick all chapters ticks the lot, the search box finds a topic, and ✕ Clear all starts again. Then press the big Continue button.

  • Step ②: your chapters are locked in at the top (🔒 Your chapters) and everything on the screen uses them: the ⚡ Eton Speed Challenge, the recap, Recap as a PDF, ▶ Start Drill, 📝 Generate Test and ⏱ Eton Spot Test: timed & submitted (choose Untimed if you like). Press ← Change chapters to go back to step ①. Item type always starts on All.

  • Press Want an Eton grammar recap?: it opens the recaps of the chapters you chose, ready to read. On that page you can also search, Tick all, or tick a group, then press Read ↓, Print or Save as PDF. Switch on Only the "Now I can…" tick boxes to see (and print) just the checklists of the chapters you ticked. Read chapters 13, 14 and 15 together in one sitting: the subjunctive, se + conditional and the passive are exactly where an A* student still slips.

  • Each recap ends with "Soon I will be able to…" and a tickable "Now I can…" checklist (about 9 to 12 statements per chapter). Say or write your own example for each statement, press Check to see the model answer, then tick it. Ticks are saved on that device only.

  • Hard items include: "Se ne avessi l'occasione, sceglierei di studiare a Roma per un anno" and "Tom ha detto che sarebbe venuto" (the conditional perfect for a future seen from the past).

Why it helps: The chapters are built from the points that learners most often get wrong, and every point is tested several ways.

1.3 Vocabulary: the 2,000 words everything else rests on

▶ Open Eton IGCSE Vocab and Gr EWs

https://apowles.github.io/AP/Eton%20IGCSE%20Vocab%20and%20Gr%20EWs.html

  • On the same page press 📚 IGCSE Vocab. Press 📚 Choose lists and tick IGCSE Nouns (1,108), IGCSE Verbs (450), IGCSE Adjectives (193) and IGCSE Prep/Conj/Adv (219).

  • Under Direction set English on the left and Italian on the right, then press ⇄ Swap and do it the other way too.

  • Tick Hard under Level. About 1,165 of the 1,970 core Italian words are Hard (nothing like the English, no clue), which is where the gaps hide. Then do Medium and Easy as well: do not assume you know them.

  • Answers show the gender and useful extras, for example "l'arte (f) (l'artista (m/f), artistico/a)". Check yours.

  • The E and D lists (E Mich Voc 2 to 10, D Lent Voc and so on) are the same words in smaller slices, useful when you have ten minutes.

Why it helps: Gender, articles and the small words (prepositions, conjunctions, adverbs) are where marks are quietly lost.

1.6 Listening: six real Cambridge papers (try one cold)

▶ Open Eton IGCSE Italian Listening

https://apowles.github.io/AP/Eton%20IGCSE%20Italian%20Listening.html

  • Open Eton IGCSE Italian Listening. It holds six real Cambridge IGCSE Italian listening papers, IT001 to IT006 (7164/12, 2021 to 2026), 37 questions and 40 marks each.

  • Do one cold, with no preparation, and see what you miss. The voice is computer-generated Italian, not the original recording, so judge the content rather than the accent.

  • Mark yourself, then drill the words and structures you missed in Eton IGCSE Vocab and Gr EWs (task 1.3).

Why it helps: Careful listening to fast, ordinary Italian shows up gaps in vocabulary and verb forms that reading never does.

2.1 Verbs: 35 specialist verbs and 613 structures

▶ Open Eton French Verbs

https://apowles.github.io/AP/Eton%20French%20Verbs.html

  • Set Verb set to Specialist. The 35 verbs include: acquérir, naître, croître, mentir, peindre, valoir, croire, rompre, fuir, craindre, apercevoir, cueillir, joindre, enfreindre, nuire, haïr, mourir, s'asseoir, vivre, se taire, atteindre, s'essuyer, pleuvoir, résoudre, moudre, coudre, vêtir, geindre, éteindre, vaincre, découvrir, décevoir, tenir, souffrir, mettre.

  • Choose All Tenses, Mode Full (6 forms), and say the whole conjugation. The tense list goes up to the subjunctive, pluperfect, future perfect and conditional perfect.

  • Then switch Verb set to 🧩 Eton Structures: "au cas où (+ CONDITIONAL)", "de peur que (+ NE + SUBJ)", "à peine étaient-ils partis que le bébé a commencé à pleurer", "quelques raisons qu'il ait avancées", "il aurait mieux valu faire X".

  • Do not skip the Quizlet set (about 100 everyday verbs in all the tenses). Everyday verbs are where slips happen.

Why it helps: The tense is chosen for you, so there is nowhere to hide on the awkward verbs.

2.2 Grammar: 20 chapters, 2,400 items, and 12 sentence sets

▶ Open Eton A Level French VVs & Gr EWs & Translation

https://apowles.github.io/AP/Eton%20A%20Level%20French%20VVs%20%26%20Gr%20EWs%20%26%20Grammar%20Translation%20Practice%20and%20Drills.html

  • Press the top-bar button 🏆 Gr EWs. The 20 chapters run from present and imperfect formation, through pronouns, negatives, the passive and relative pronouns, to the subjunctive (chapters 15 and 16), word order and inversion (17), articles, adjectives and adverbs.

  • Under the chapter drop-down there is a bar "Want an Eton grammar recap? Gr N · title". Read it first. It has a colour key (rules, examples, tips and patterns, exceptions, watch out, summaries), mnemonics such as ANPQ and PJRP, and ends with "Soon I will be able to…" and a "Now I can…" checklist of 12 statements per chapter. Try to do each one before you press Check.

  • To read or tick through several chapters together, press Grammar recaps: pick one or several (under the same bar). Tick the chapters you want, then press Read ↓ or 🖨 Print / Save as PDF. Switch on Only the "Now I can…" tick boxes to get just the checklists of those chapters.

  • Choose Test me on: Translations only to practise producing French, or Rules only for pure recall. Tick several chapters to combine them.

  • Press 🎯 Grammar Drills for 12 sets of 88 sentences each: the passive, past participle agreement, the subjunctive, si clauses, object pronouns, relative pronouns, imparfait v passé composé, negatives, the gerund, depuis and venir de, rendre X adj and faire inf, infinitive constructions.

  • Each Grammar Drills sentence has the grammar point in bold: write only those words in French. The answer shows the whole French sentence with that part highlighted. Tests and the Speed Challenge work the same way, and a timed test counts these as short answers (20 items).

Why it helps: Each point is tested again and again in different sentences, so errors stand out.

Part 3. A routine, and how the drills work

Five 15-minute sessions a week

Day Task Where
Monday Italian verbs: irregular, passato remoto and subjunctives (20 cards, then drill the errors) Eton Italian Verbs
Tuesday French verbs: Specialist verbs, all tenses Eton French Verbs
Wednesday Italian grammar: read the recap for one chapter (13 to 15 first, then 1 to 10), drill it, then tick its "Now I can…" list Eton IGCSE Vocab and Gr EWs
Thursday French grammar: read the recap, do a Gr EWs chapter or Grammar Drills set, tick "Now I can…", then one translation passage Eton A Level French VVs & Gr EWs & Translation
Friday Vocabulary, Hard first: Italian nouns and verbs, then French Eton IGCSE Vocab and Gr EWs
Any day Leisure Mode while walking, Speed Challenge with a friend any app

How a drill works

  1. Open the app. Choose what to practise and press ▶ Start Drill. The card is read aloud.

  2. Say the answer out loud first. Then press Show Answer (or the → key).

  3. Mark yourself: ✗ Major error (←), ~ Nearly (↓) or ✓ Got it (→). Be strict. A missing accent is "Nearly".

  4. At the end press ▶ Drill major errors again. Use Copy list to paste your errors into Notes.

Things worth knowing

  • Sound: the voice reading each card is your device's text-to-speech. It sounds much better with an enhanced voice installed (on a Mac or iPhone: Settings, Accessibility, Spoken Content). Tap 🔊 Voices in any app to check.

  • Scores are not saved. Use Copy list to keep the cards you got wrong.

  • Ignore: the round photo in the header, any button marked ✎ Edit, Print Content or 🔑, and the "Save my work as PDF" and OneNote steps at the end of timed tests. Those belong to your teacher's classes.

  • Tests: 📝 Generate Test makes a printable test. ⏱ Eton Spot Test: timed & submitted is a short on-screen test that you mark yourself (choose Untimed to take it at your own pace). In a timed test, do not leave the page: a loud siren sounds. If something urgent happens (a fire alarm, for example), press ⏸ Pause at the top right, type why, then press ▶ Resume; the pause is printed on your PDF. While a timed test is open your iPad stays awake (it will not dim or lock itself); if you lock it yourself with the side button, that is logged like leaving the page. If the clock at the top of the screen distracts you, tap it to make it smaller (tap again to make it bigger); it stays on screen. When you mark yourself, use red pen on your answer and then tap a mark; if the red reminders to tap your mark are too much, press 🔕 Turn off the red “tap your mark” alerts (press 🔔 Turn the red alerts back on to bring them back). An unmarked answer still scores 0. When you have saved your PDF you can press 🔄 Take a fresh test at the bottom of the page for new questions with the same settings (the test you have just done is cleared, so save its PDF first). No timed test runs longer than 4½ minutes: a test of short answers (words or verb forms) can have up to 20 items, while longer answers give fewer. Where your teacher's list gives more than one accepted answer for an English prompt (for example "living room": la salle de séjour / le salon), the mark scheme now shows them all. Spellings that are right either way are listed too (for example l'aller simple, with or without the hyphen).

  • Levels: most apps have Easy / Medium / Hard. Easy looks like English, Medium looks like a different English word, Hard gives you nothing to go on. Start on Hard to find the gaps, then clear the rest.
